## The comparison groups

The results pages show a run beside two groups of people who contributed results, as they described
themselves when they did:

{groups}

Only sessions where both tasks reached a threshold are counted. People who answered "prefer not to
say" about psychedelics are in neither group, and so is anyone who reported a visual perceptual
disorder. Groups are kept separately for each version of the test and for the full and quick tests,
and a run is only shown beside groups from its own. A group isn't shown until at least {minGroup}
people are in it: fewer would mean little, and couldn't keep individuals hidden. For each group the
pages show the median, the middle half and the middle 80% of results. Results taken in a browser
vary with screens, lighting and attention, and people describe themselves, so the groups are
information only: nothing is concluded from where a result falls.

## Taking the test more than once

Repeated testing could lower thresholds without any other change, through familiarity with the task
and through perceptual learning. In the lab, coherence thresholds for dynamic Glass patterns fell
over eight daily sessions of 600 trials, fastest in the first sessions, and the learning transferred
to random-dot motion without form cues (Donato et al. 2024). Training on global motion direction
improved performance over five days, but only with feedback on every trial: without it, ten days of
training brought no significant change (Asher et al. 2019). This test gives feedback only on its
practice trials, and a session is far shorter than those studies' training, so how much repeating
it affects results isn't known. Familiarity with the task probably affects both tasks alike and so
partly cancels in motion − form. Perceptual learning might not, since in both studies how far it
transferred depended on the stimulus. The groups pool every contributed session without removing
repeats, so one practised person can contribute several sessions to a group. In the public export,
sessions sent with the same pseudonym can be linked, so repeats can be found or left out; sessions
sent without a pseudonym can't be linked.
