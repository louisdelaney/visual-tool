Vision research prototype

Form–Motion Coherence Test

Two short tasks from vision research. Each finds your coherence threshold, the smallest share of organised dots you can still pick out from random ones. One task uses moving dots, the other a pattern hidden among random dots.

    What you'll see

    Before you start

    • A laptop or desktop with a keyboard. Phones and tablets won't work.
    • A bank card, or any card the same size, to measure your screen.
    • About 10 minutes in a dim room, sitting about an arm's length (50 cm) from the screen.
    • Your usual glasses or contact lenses.

    Please read. This is a research prototype, not a medical test. It can't diagnose HPPD, visual snow or any other condition, and there's no reference data for results taken in a browser yet. The tasks show moving and flickering dots: don't run them if you have photosensitive epilepsy, and press Esc to stop if they make you feel unwell. Results stay in this browser unless you save or copy them.

    This window is too small to run the tasks. Open the page on a laptop or desktop and maximise the window. You can still look at the example results.

    Measure your screen

    Hold a bank card, or any card the same size, flat against the screen over the outline. Move the slider until the outline is exactly as wide as the card.

    ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 · 85.60 × 53.98 mm

    The outline is set to the size saved on this browser. Check it still matches your card.

    Don't change the browser zoom after this step, and keep the window on this screen.

    Measure your viewing distance

    The tasks are specified in degrees of visual angle, so the page needs to know how far your eyes are from the screen. It works this out from your blind spot, which sits about 13.5° from wherever you look. Sit centred in front of the screen, as you will for the tasks: the maths assumes it.
